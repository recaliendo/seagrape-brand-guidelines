Brand foundation

Yards that can take the South Florida sun

Why the name: the seagrape (Coccoloba uvifera) is a native coastal plant you see all over Broward, from the dunes at Hollywood Beach to backyard hedges in Plantation. It handles salt, heat and hurricane wind. That's the promise we want customers to feel.

Positioning

The reliable local crew that shows up on the day we said, keeps your yard healthy through wet season, and knows which plants actually survive here.

Who we serve

  • Busy homeowners (30–60) in single-family homes who want "set it and forget it" weekly service.
  • New Florida transplants who don't know St. Augustine from Zoysia and need a guide.
  • Seasonal residents who need the yard kept up while they're away.
  • Small HOAs and landlords once the crew can handle volume.

Voice & tone

Plain, friendly, specific

Write short sentences. Name days, times and prices when you can. Skip words like "premier," "elevate" and "solutions."

Say thisWe mow every Tuesday morning. If a storm pushes us back, we'll text you the new day.
Not thisElevate your outdoor living experience with our premier landscaping solutions.
Say thisFree quote in 24 hours. Weekly lawn care starts at $45 for most Pembroke Pines lots.
Not thisContact us today to learn more about our competitive pricing!

Bilingual from day one

A large share of Broward households speak Spanish at home, and Portuguese and Haitian Creole are common too. At minimum, offer key pages and the quote form in Spanish, say "Hablamos español" in the header if true, and have a native speaker review translations. Don't rely on auto-translate widgets.

Logo

The seagrape leaf

A nearly round leaf with the plant's signature red veins. Simple enough to read on a truck door at 40 mph or as a 32px browser icon.

Versions

  • Primary: horizontal lockup on light backgrounds
  • Reversed: on Seagrape green or photos
  • Mark only: favicon, social avatar, hats, stickers
  • One-color: all Mangrove or all white for vinyl and embroidery

Spacing & size

  • Clear space on all sides: half the leaf's height
  • Minimum lockup width: 140px on screen, 1.5 in printed
  • Mark alone: 24px minimum

Don't

  • Stretch, rotate or add shadows
  • Put the green logo on busy foliage photos
  • Swap in a palm tree. Every Florida company uses one.
  • Recolor the veins anything but red, green or yellow

Where it shows up first

For a startup landscaper, the truck, yard signs and crew shirts bring in more leads than the website. Design those before anything else: truck door (lockup plus phone number in 4-inch type), corrugated yard sign ("Another yard by Seagrape" plus phone), and Seagrape-green polos or sun shirts with the mark on the chest.

Typography

Sturdy headlines, easy-reading body

Two free Google Fonts. Bricolage Grotesque gives headlines a hand-built, friendly feel. Atkinson Hyperlegible was designed for readability, which helps older homeowners reading prices on a phone outdoors.

Rules: body text never below 16px. Headlines in sentence case. Prices and phone numbers in bold body font, never in light weights.

Photography & icons

Real yards, real crew, real Florida

Your own photos beat stock every time. A phone camera is fine if you follow a few rules.

Shoot

  • Before and after from the exact same spot and angle
  • Morning or late afternoon. Noon sun flattens everything.
  • Crew in branded shirts and hats, working
  • Fresh edges, striped St. Augustine, clean mulch beds, sabal palms against blue sky
  • The truck and trailer, parked clean, logo visible

Avoid

  • Stock photos of northern lawns, fall leaves, tulips or picket fences
  • House numbers, license plates or customers' faces without permission
  • Heavy filters or over-saturated green grass
  • Photos of mid-job mess

Icons

Use simple line icons with 2px strokes and rounded ends (Lucide or Phosphor are free). Color them Seagrape on light backgrounds and Sawgrass on dark. Use one icon style across the site; don't mix in emoji or clip art.

Technical must-haves

  • Mobile-first; phone number is tap-to-call everywhere
  • Pages load in under 2.5 seconds; compress images to WebP
  • Name, address and phone identical on the site, Google Business Profile, Nextdoor and Yelp
  • LocalBusiness schema markup with service area
  • Form submissions go to your phone by text, not just email
  • Alt text on every photo ("Fresh mulch bed with firebush in Davie")

Launch checklist

Before you go live

  • Final business name checked on Sunbiz.org and as a domain
  • Logo files exported: SVG, PNG (transparent), one-color
  • Google Business Profile claimed and verified
  • 20+ real job photos, including 3 before/after pairs
  • Truck magnets or wrap and 25 yard signs printed
  • Spanish versions of Home, Services and Quote pages
  • Quote form tested on iPhone and Android
  • Insurance and license info ready to display
  • First 5 customers asked for a Google review
  • Hurricane-prep landing page ready by May 15
